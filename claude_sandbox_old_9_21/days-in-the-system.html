Days in the System

National figures on time spent in each HUD project type, what happens between enrollments, and how often clients use more than one program in a year.

Compiled 10 Sep 2026 2022 AHAR Part 2 · CoC SPM data FY2019–FY2024 Figures computed from the source files

Three things to know before the tables

HUD does not publish a mean or median length of stay by project type. The AHAR Part 2 reports duration only as a bucketed distribution (≤7 days, 1 week–1 month, and so on). The only national figure expressed in days is SPM Measure 1, and that is a person-level count of cumulative homelessness across ES/SH/TH combined — not a project-level stay.

Nothing published measures the gap between one enrollment and the next. The closest national proxy is SPM Measure 2, returns to homelessness at 6, 12 and 24 months after an exit, broken out by the project type exited.

Overlapping enrollments are published — as annual co-occurrence, not simultaneity. The AHAR reports which combinations of project types a household touched during the year, which is the closest thing to your question and is genuinely useful. Same-day overlap is treated by HUD as a data quality error, not a measured phenomenon.

The shape of it

Adult-only households are twice as likely as families to leave within a week (26% vs 15%), but families are more likely to be there past six months (25% vs 19%). Unaccompanied youth are the shortest stayers by a wide margin — 59% are out within a month and only 4% stay a year or more. Chronically homeless adult-only households are the longest — 30% past 180 days, and 15% past a year. Veterans leave quickly less often than the general adult-only population (16% vs 26% within a week), a pattern consistent with structured VA-linked programs rather than night-by-night shelter.

RRH stays lengthened sharply through the pandemic and had not come back: the share of households using RRH for three months or less fell from 44% in 2019 to 34% in 2022, while the share using it for 18 months or longer rose from under 5% to over 10%. Between enrollments

No national dataset reports days elapsed between an exit from one project type and an entry into the next. What exists is the return rate, by the project type exited — the published measure that comes closest to your question.

Roughly half of all two-year returns happen within the first six months across every exit type — the risk is heavily front-loaded, which is the practical answer to "how long until they come back." Exits from permanent housing are about half as likely to produce a return as exits from shelter, at every horizon.

Data quality warning on FY2024

In the FY2024 sheet, the transitional-housing return block is broken: 231 of 385 CoCs report non-zero returns at 6 months but zero at 12 and 24 months, which is arithmetically impossible since the longer cohorts are supposed to include the shorter ones. FY2019–FY2023 pass this check cleanly for every project type. Use FY2023 as the latest usable year for Measure 2 transitional housing, and treat published FY2024 TH return rates as unreliable.

For the timing rules themselves, the two national reports do not agree, which matters if you are reconciling your own gaps against a published figure.

LSA / Stella

Seven-day rule

Two dates of homelessness are continuous as long as they are separated by fewer than seven days. Sub-week gaps don't split an episode.

SPM

Contiguous only

Only consecutive dates count as one episode. A single day's gap is a break. LSA day counts therefore reach further back than SPM ones for the same client.

Chronic definition

Seven consecutive nights

A break between occasions counts only if it includes at least 7 consecutive nights not homeless — a third seven-day threshold, with its own rules.

AHAR returns

Fourteen days

The AHAR counts a "return" only after 14 days with no homeless services, within a two-year window — different again from both of the above.

LSA / Stella

Duration thresholds

Longstayer at 180+ cumulative days, long-term homeless at 365+. Use these if you need standard buckets.

LSA vs SPM

Unit of analysis

LSA follows households via the head of household and breaks out household type; SPM counts all persons served with no such breakdowns.

One structural mismatch to plan around

HUD's "system pathway" in Stella is not a sequence — it is the distinct combination of project types a household was enrolled in during the period, so ES→TH→RRH and RRH→TH→ES land in the same bucket. Ordered A→B transitions with a mean gap on each edge are your own construct, and no published national comparator exists for them.

The asymmetry is worth noticing. Looked at from the shelter side, multi-program use looks rare — 93% of shelter households touched nothing else. Looked at from the RRH side it is the norm for a large minority: a quarter of RRH households also used a shelter program that year, rising to 28% for adult-only households. Families are the mirror image, far more likely to pair shelter with RRH (10% vs 4%) but far less likely to combine RRH with anything once housed (81% used RRH alone).

And PSH is where the entry pathway shows: 6.4% of PSH households also used shelter in the same year, which is largely people moving into PSH rather than cycling.

Same-day overlaps, and what HUD says about them

Simultaneous enrollment is treated as a data quality problem rather than something to be measured. The LSA applies a 1% tolerance threshold to allow for genuinely rare legitimate cases and expects the rest to be cleaned before submission.

How concurrent enrollments are treated
OverlapTreatmentNotes
Same project, same person Never permitted An entry-to-exit overlap within one ProjectID is invalid for every project type, no exceptions.
ES ↔ SH ↔ TH Error "In general, we do not expect overlapping days in project among and between the following project types: Emergency shelter, Safe haven, or Transitional housing." Includes entry/exit stays overlapping night-by-night bed nights elsewhere.
Residential ↔ PH after move-in ≈99% error A shelter enrollment left open past a housing move-in date. The classic cleaning target.
PH ↔ PH ≈99% error Overlapping move-in dates across two PSH projects, or RRH and PSH held simultaneously.
CE ↔ residential Undefined The real gap. Neither the LSA data quality guidance nor the CoC HMIS Manual addresses CE running concurrently with ES/TH/RRH/PSH. HUD describes CE as "a collaborative and community-wide process and not necessarily represented by a single project," and defines no standard CE start or exit. CE contributes no homeless days, so an overlap causes no double-counting — which is why most implementations permit it and HUD never had to rule. Practice varies by CoC; no national benchmark exists.
HP ↔ anything Undefined Also unaddressed, and for the same reason: HP days are not homeless days.

If you're computing this yourself

  • Report distributions, not means, if you want to compare to national. HUD's buckets are 7 days, 1 month, 3, 6, 12, 18 months, 2 years, 3 years. Matching them costs nothing and makes your figures directly comparable to the AHAR exhibits above.
  • Pick your seven-day rule deliberately. There are three in play — LSA continuity (<7 days), the chronic-homelessness break (≥7 consecutive nights), and the AHAR return threshold (14 days). They are not interchangeable.
  • Decide between entry-to-entry and exit-to-entry gaps. Both go negative wherever enrollments legitimately overlap, which they do for CE; exit-to-entry additionally breaks on open enrollments with no exit date.
  • Mirror LSA logic for anything longitudinal. Household-level, RRH and PSH kept separate, sub-week gaps tolerated — closer to what a client timeline wants than SPM's person-level contiguous-days rule.
  • Expect a quarter of your RRH clients to also show a shelter enrollment in the same year, and about 6% of PSH clients likewise. If your data shows far less, suspect a join problem before concluding your system is unusual.
